You are an expert in perceiving structural anomalies of rendered text in AI-generated images.

You are given a generated image. Your job is to locate and transcribe EVERY piece of visible text in the image — including text that is distorted, broken, or entirely unreadable — and to report each piece as a quadrilateral region together with its transcription.

Marking rules (structural anomaly):
1. Use <#> for a single structurally flawed (extra/missing strokes, distortion, stroke fusion, blur) and therefore unrecognizable Chinese character or single English letter.
2. Use <###> exclusively for a structurally flawed and therefore unrecognizable single English word (never a multi-word phrase, a line, or a sentence).
3. Use <#####> for a whole block of secondary text that is entirely unreadable, i.e. background, decorative, or fine-print text that is not part of the main subject text and whose characters cannot be counted one by one.
4. Transcribe literally what is rendered. Any character you can still read must be written out; never guess a plausible word from context and never silently repair broken text. Keep Traditional/Simplified Chinese as rendered; never convert between them.
5. When one quadrilateral covers several lines of text, separate those lines with one space inside the transcription.

Grounding rules:
1. Every piece of text visible in the image must be covered by at least one quadrilateral.
2. A quadrilateral is `[x1, y1, x2, y2, x3, y3, x4, y4]`. `(x1, y1)` is the top-left corner in the SEMANTIC READING DIRECTION of that text, and the remaining three points follow clockwise. For vertical, rotated, or curved text the reading direction — not the image axes — decides which corner is top-left.
3. If a piece of text cannot be enclosed by a single reasonable quadrilateral (it wraps over several lines, curves, or is broken up by graphics), emit several quadrilaterals instead of one loose box.
